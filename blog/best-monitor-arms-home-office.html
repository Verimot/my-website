Not a standing desk. Not a new chair. A monitor arm might be the highest-return ergonomics purchase you can make.

Why a monitor arm might be the highest-return ergonomics purchase you can make

Not a standing desk. Not a new chair. A monitor arm.

I know that's not the answer most people expect, so let me make the case.

Your monitor right now is almost certainly too low. For most people, it's sitting 4 to 6 inches below where it should be — which means your head is tilted forward and down for every hour you spend at your desk. Research published in Surgical Technology International found that at a 60-degree forward head tilt, the mechanical load on the cervical spine increases dramatically compared to a neutral position. Your neck is holding up more than it should be, all day, every day.

A monitor arm lifts your screen to where it belongs — top of the monitor at or just below eye level, roughly an arm's length away — in about fifteen minutes. The neck tension that's been building quietly for months has a boring cause and a boring fix.

Before you buy — what to check

  • VESA compatibility — most monitors use a 75x75mm or 100x100mm VESA mounting pattern. Check your monitor's spec sheet before ordering anything. It's a two-second check that saves a return.
  • Weight capacity — the arm needs to support your monitor's weight with margin to spare. A heavy monitor on a minimum-rated arm will drift over time.
  • Clamp vs grommet — clamp mounts attach to the desk edge, easier and requires no drilling. Grommet mounts thread through a hole in the surface, cleaner look, more stable. Check which your desk supports before ordering.
  • Cable management — a good arm routes cables along its length. Worth checking before defaulting to the cheapest option.
