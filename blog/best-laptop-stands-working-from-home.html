The Best Laptop Stands for Working From Home — The Fix Most People Are Missing

Working on a flat laptop is doing damage you haven't noticed yet. Here's the fix — and which stand to buy.

By Deskfix  ·  2026  ·  5 min read

Working on a flat laptop is doing damage you haven't noticed yet

This isn't hyperbole. It's just slow enough that most people don't connect it to the cause.

When your laptop sits flat on a desk, the screen lands somewhere between 4 and 6 inches below where your eyes should actually be. To see it, your head tilts forward. You do this all day without thinking about it. Your neck muscles hold that position under load for hours. Over weeks and months, the tension accumulates into the kind of chronic upper back stiffness that most laptop workers have just accepted as part of how they feel.

A laptop stand raises the screen to eye level. It's not complicated. The total cost of this fix — stand plus the external keyboard you'll need — is less than most people spend on desk gear that does nothing for their posture.

One thing to know before buying

Once your laptop is elevated, the built-in keyboard is too high to type on comfortably — your wrists would be angled upward, which creates a different problem. You need an external keyboard positioned at elbow height. Factor that into your budget alongside the stand. A decent wireless keyboard doesn't cost much and it completes the setup properly.

The complete setup — three pieces that work together

  • Laptop stand — screen to eye level
  • External keyboard — wrists at the correct typing height
  • External mouse — less wrist strain than sustained trackpad use

These three items together address the most common causes of upper body pain in laptop users. If you're making one change to your setup this week, elevating your screen is where to start.
